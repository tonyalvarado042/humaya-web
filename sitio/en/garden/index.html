The garden

From soil to plate,
no stops.

The shortest distance between what grows and what you eat. A garden on the same land where you sleep, and two ways into it: walking or cooking.

Everyone says «farm to table». Here it's thirty metres.

The phrase gets used for everything: usually it means someone buys from a local producer, which is already good. At Humaya it means something simpler and more literal: the garden is on the property, you can walk over and see it, and what comes out of it reaches the kitchen the same day.

01

Go and see it

It isn't hidden behind the kitchen. You can walk in, ask what everything is, and take something with you.

02

Cook it

Cooking classes with whatever is ready that day. No experience needed, and the idea is that you leave with something you can make again at home.

03

Eat it

What isn't used in the class reaches the plate anyway. It's the Nourish pillar in its shortest form.

Walks

You walk out from the door.

In La Fortuna almost everything is done by car: the volcano, the hot springs, the waterfalls, the hanging bridges. That's fine, and worth it. But there's a difference between visiting nature and being inside it.

Here the trails start at the hotel. You can head out for a walk before breakfast without arranging anything with anyone.

The five pillars

Frequently asked questions.

Do I need cooking experience?
No. The classes are designed so that someone who has never cooked leaves with something they can make again at home.
Do the walks start at the hotel?
Yes. The trails start on the property itself: no driving and no transport to book in order to go for a walk.
Can I visit the garden whenever I want?
Yes. It isn't locked or hidden: it's part of the place, not a backdrop for photos.
When does Humaya open?
In December 2026, in La Fortuna de San Carlos, Costa Rica. The first reservations can already be made online.
